# Project context

This is m60motorway.com: a map of Manchester's M60 and its 27 junctions. It is a dependency-free static site. There is no build step for the
page; only the map data is generated.

## What is here

- `index.html` is the page: a full-window map with a floating title, tools and a panel. The panel is the ring as a list: all 27 junctions grouped by stretch, with the chosen one opened in place to show its signs. An ⓘ button swaps the list for About: the illustrated scene of the Barton High Level Bridge, "Good to know" (the road's quirks, with links back to their junctions) and its history.
- `styles.css` holds the whole visual system, both themes and the responsive layout.
- `script.js` draws the map from `data/geo.js`, and handles pan and zoom, the junction list, About and "Drive the loop".
- `data/junctions.js` holds the words: each junction's place, name, sign destinations for both directions, and note, the stretches that group the list, and the "Good to know" items shown under About.
- `data/geo.js` is generated by `scripts/build_geo.py` from the OpenStreetMap extracts in `scripts/osm/`. Do not edit it by hand.
- `scripts/build_artifact.py` bundles everything into one file for a Claude artifact preview.
- `CNAME` and `.nojekyll` are for GitHub Pages.

## Design intent

The page is one screen with no page scroll and no header bar: everything floats
over the map, and the panel is one plain list rather than tabs. Keep list rows to
number, place and roads; quirks and trivia belong in a junction's note or under
About, not in the list. On phones the panel is
a bottom sheet that collapses to its top bar. The map is fitted into the space the panel leaves, so the ring is never
hidden behind it. The site is a reference, not a learning tool; don't add quiz
or memorisation features.

The look follows David's personal site, dejones.io: warm paper, green-black ink,
Lora for display and DM Sans for UI, soft shadows, generous space, and a
hand-drawn scene. The M60 itself is drawn in dusk rose; the selected junction and
its next stretch are gold. Avoid road-sign blue and industrial styling.

Keep both themes coherent. The theme is stored in `localStorage` under `theme`
and applied as `data-theme` on `<html>` before first paint. Keep reduced motion
working: the bridge scene's animations pause, and the map's animated zoom is
skipped.

Map symbols (tokens, labels, tags, the car) are drawn at a constant screen size
by scaling them with the map's units-per-pixel on every layout. Tokens are pushed
apart by a small relaxation so none overlap; labels that would collide with a
token, a tag or the road are hidden rather than moved.

## Facts

Junction facts come from the Wikipedia junction list (driver location signs) and
OpenStreetMap junction names. Do not invent destinations. If a fact is uncertain,
leave it out.

## Working locally

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8765
```

Before handing off a visual change, check light and dark themes, a phone width
and a desktop width, the browser console, and reduced motion.
